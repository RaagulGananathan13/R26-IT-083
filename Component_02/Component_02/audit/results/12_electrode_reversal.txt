==============================================================================
  6. WHAT DETECTION SENSITIVITY WOULD ACTUALLY BE ENOUGH?
==============================================================================
  Detection at 70% sensitivity restored only 1 of 9 guarantees, because the
  30% of reversed records that slip through are enough to keep the bound
  void. So the useful question is not 'can we detect it' but 'how well must
  we detect it, given how often it happens?'

  With a reversal prevalence r and detector sensitivity s, the residual
  contaminated fraction is r(1-s), and the realised miss rate is
      m(s) = [1 - r(1-s)] * m_correct  +  r(1-s) * m_reversed
  Solving m(s) <= alpha gives the sensitivity a deployment must achieve.

  Reported prevalence of limb-lead reversal in clinical practice spans
  roughly 0.4% to 4%. Required sensitivity at each:

  class  variant    alpha      r=0.4%      r=1.0%      r=2.0%      r=4.0%
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  NORM   RA/LA       0.20          0%          0%          0%          0%
  NORM   RA/LL       0.20          0%          0%          0%          0%
  STTC   RA/LA       0.10  impossible  impossible  impossible  impossible
  STTC   RA/LL       0.10  impossible  impossible  impossible  impossible
  CD     LA/LL       0.10          0%          0%          0%          0%
  HYP    RA/LA       0.15  impossible  impossible  impossible  impossible
  HYP    RA/LL       0.15  impossible  impossible  impossible  impossible

  Read a cell as: 'at this reversal prevalence, a detector must reach this
  sensitivity before the promised bound is honoured again.'
  '0%' means the correct-placement miss rate already leaves enough slack.
  'impossible' means the bound is already violated on correctly placed ECGs.

  THE RESULT IS MOSTLY '0%', AND THAT IS THE FINDING.

  At a realistic prevalence of 0.4-4%, the POPULATION-level guarantee
  survives electrode reversal: 96-99.6% of records are correctly placed, so
  the marginal miss rate stays inside its bound. A hospital auditing this
  system across all its ECGs would see nothing wrong.

  But for the INDIVIDUAL PATIENT whose electrodes were swapped, the promise
  is void — the miss rate on their record is the reversed-column figure
  above, not the advertised alpha — and no part of the system tells anyone.

  This is the SAME failure mode as the subgroup result in
  10_conditional_validity.py, on a second and independent axis:

      marginal validity holds        |  conditional validity fails
      ----------------------------------------------------------------
      across the whole population    |  within a patient subgroup (age)
      across all recordings          |  within a mis-acquired recording

  One principle, two demonstrations: a conformal guarantee averaged over a
  population says nothing about the patient in front of you. The remedy is
  the same in both cases — condition the guarantee on what actually varies,
  by patient stratum (Mondrian) and by verified acquisition (this module).

  Note the 'impossible' rows: STTC's miss rate on CORRECTLY placed ECGs is
  already 11.4% against a promised 10%, so no detector can rescue it. That
  cell needs recalibration, not better acquisition checks.
