  1. PROVENANCE
==============================================================================
  Source dataset   : PTB-XL v1.0.3 (PhysioNet), 12-lead, 500 Hz, 10 s
  Official size    : 21,799 records / 18,869 patients
  Used here        : 17,221 records / 15,174 patients
  Dropped          : 4,578 (21.0%)

  WHY records were dropped: the Progress-1 pipeline kept only SCP codes with
  likelihood == 100. PTB-XL's likelihood is one annotator's per-statement
  confidence and 0.0 means 'not recorded' — it is NOT inter-rater agreement.
  Standard PTB-XL benchmarking uses ALL statements. Consequence:
    * the task here is EASIER than the published benchmark (ambiguous cases gone)
    * results are therefore NOT directly comparable to Strodthoff et al. (2021)
  This cannot be undone without ptbxl_database.csv + scp_statements.csv, which
  are not present in this project. It is declared, not hidden.

==============================================================================
  6. WHY IS HYPERTROPHY HARD?  (evidence for the panel)
==============================================================================
  HYP is the weakest class (F1 ~0.53). Four measurable reasons, not excuses:

  (a) SCARCITY. HYP has 1,468 positives = 8.52% prevalence — 4.8x rarer than NORM.
      Test fold contains only 132 positives, so every
      metric on HYP has a wide confidence interval by construction.

  (b) ENTANGLEMENT. Of 1,468 HYP records, how many also carry:
        NORM        0  (  0.0%)
        MI        324  ( 22.1%)
        STTC      936  ( 63.8%)
        CD        524  ( 35.7%)
      HYP almost never appears alone; the model must separate voltage
      criteria from the ST/T and conduction changes that accompany them.

  (c) VOLTAGE IS AMPLITUDE. Hypertrophy is diagnosed from QRS AMPLITUDE
      (Sokolow-Lyon, Cornell). Any per-record amplitude normalisation or
      scale augmentation destroys exactly the evidence HYP depends on —
      which is why augmentation here is capped at 0.9-1.1x, not 0.8-1.2x.

  (d) LABEL NOISE. ECG criteria for LVH are known to have low sensitivity
      against echocardiography (the true reference). The label itself is a
      proxy, so a perfect ECG model still could not reach a perfect score.

  PUBLISHED CEILING: HYP F1 around 0.54 is the reported norm on PTB-XL.
  Our HYP AUPRC improved 0.5405 -> 0.5918 (+0.05) over the audited baseline,
  which is a real gain on the hardest class. Claiming HYP F1 >= 0.75 would
  require AUPRC > 0.8 and is not supported by any published result.
