================================================================================
SECTION 1 — MOTIVE (WHY THIS PROJECT EXISTS)
================================================================================

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death globally, responsible for
approximately 17.9 million deaths per year (WHO, 2023). Electrocardiograms (ECGs)
are the single most widely used tool for diagnosing cardiac conditions. A standard
12-lead ECG can reveal life-threatening abnormalities such as myocardial infarction,
bundle branch blocks, and ventricular hypertrophy in seconds.

THE PROBLEM:
  - Skilled cardiologists are scarce in rural hospitals, primary care clinics,
    and developing countries.
  - ECG interpretation is highly specialist-dependent and time-sensitive.
  - Manual report writing is slow, inconsistent, and prone to human fatigue.
  - Existing AI tools are black boxes — clinicians cannot verify WHY the system
    made a particular decision.
  - Free-text AI generation models (LLMs) hallucinate clinical findings that do
    not exist in the signal, creating a patient safety risk.

THE MOTIVATION:
  To build a clinically trustworthy, explainable, automated ECG analysis system
  that can:
    1. Classify 12-lead ECG signals into 5 standard diagnostic superclasses.
    2. Generate a grounded, hallucination-free clinical report.
    3. Explain WHICH part of the ECG signal and WHICH leads drove the diagnosis.
    4. Be usable by a non-specialist via a web browser with zero setup.

SECTION 2 — WHOLE IDEA (THE BIG PICTURE)
================================================================================

The system works end-to-end as follows:

  STEP 1 — A clinical ECG signal is provided.
    Either: selected from the PTB-XL test set via an interactive browser
    Or:     uploaded as a raw .dat + .hea WFDB file by the user

  STEP 2 — The signal is preprocessed.
    The 12-lead, 500Hz, 10-second signal (5000 samples per lead) is normalized
    using per-lead mean and standard deviation computed from the training set.

  STEP 3 — The ResNet CNN Classifier runs.
    A custom 1D ResNet architecture processes all 12 leads simultaneously and
    outputs 5 sigmoid probabilities — one per diagnostic superclass.
    Per-class optimal decision thresholds (tuned on validation set) convert
    probabilities into binary predictions.

  STEP 4 — Explainability (XAI) is computed.
    Grad-CAM is applied to the last ResNet layer to highlight which TIME REGIONS
    of the ECG were most important for the prediction.
    Integrated Gradients are applied per-lead to rank which LEADS (I, II, V1...V6)
    contributed most to the classification.

  STEP 5 — A clinical report is generated (3-tier hybrid pipeline).
    Tier 1: CNN probabilities (structured classification output)
    Tier 2: Template Engine — fills pre-written clinical sentence slots based on
            what the CNN detected. Zero hallucination risk.
    Tier 3: BioBART Smoother — takes the Tier 2 structured text and rewrites it
            in natural clinical prose. It cannot invent new findings because it
            only sees the template text, not the raw signal.
    Legacy: BioBART Free Generation — kept for research comparison only.

  STEP 6 — Results are displayed on the web interface.
    - ECG waveform plot with Grad-CAM heatmap overlay
    - Per-class probability bars with detected/not detected status
    - Lead importance ranking
    - Three report variants (Template, Smoothed, Legacy)
    - Ground-truth labels for comparison (test set browser mode)

SECTION 3 — NOVELTY (WHAT MAKES THIS DIFFERENT)
================================================================================

NOVELTY 1 — THREE-TIER HYBRID REPORTING PIPELINE
  Most ECG AI systems either:
    (a) Output only class labels (no report), or
    (b) Use a free-text LLM that hallucinates findings
  This project introduces a novel Tier 2 "classifier-first, template-second"
  architecture where the language model CANNOT hallucinate because it only receives
  the already-verified structured text, not the raw signal.

NOVELTY 2 — MULTI-MODAL FUSION FOR CLASSIFICATION
  The full training model (train.py) fuses THREE modalities:
    - 12-lead ECG signal (1D CNN ResNet)
    - Patient demographics (age, sex, height, weight — MLP encoder)
    - Clinical text report (ClinicalBERT embeddings — text projector)
  Most published ECG classifiers use signal only. This system incorporates clinical
  context and patient characteristics as additional signal.

NOVELTY 3 — EXPLAINABILITY AS A FIRST-CLASS FEATURE
  The web interface presents BOTH Grad-CAM (temporal regions) and Integrated
  Gradients (lead importance) together in a single view — not as post-hoc analysis
  but as a core output of every inference call.

NOVELTY 4 — CLINICAL HALLUCINATION RISK ANALYSIS
  The project includes a formal deep audit (audit_real_vs_generated.txt, 1711
  records) that explicitly quantifies hallucination rates per generation tier,
  comparing ROUGE-L scores and term-level analysis against cardiologist ground truth.

NOVELTY 5 — ZERO-SETUP CLINICAL DEPLOYMENT
  The system runs entirely in a browser via Flask. Clinicians can upload real
  WFDB-format ECG files (.dat + .hea) and receive a full analysis instantly
  without installing any software.

SECTION 4 — DATASET
================================================================================

NAME: PTB-XL (Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt ECG Dataset)
SOURCE: PhysioNet — https://physionet.org/content/ptb-xl/1.0.3/
CITATION: Wagner et al. (2020). Scientific Data, 7(1), 154.

SPECIFICATIONS:
  - 21,837 clinical 12-lead ECG recordings
  - Sampling rate: 500 Hz (high resolution) and 100 Hz (low resolution)
  - Duration: 10 seconds per recording (5,000 samples per lead at 500Hz)
  - Leads: I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, V1, V2, V3, V4, V5, V6
  - Patient metadata: age, sex, height, weight
  - Annotations: 71 SCP-ECG diagnostic codes assigned by 2 cardiologists each

LABEL STRUCTURE:
  71 granular SCP codes are mapped to 5 SUPERCLASSES:

    NORM  — Normal ECG (no cardiac abnormality)
              ~9,500 records (~44% of dataset)

    MI    — Myocardial Infarction
              Includes IMI, AMI, LMI, ASMI, ILMI, IPLMI, PMI, etc.
              ~5,100 records (~24%)

    STTC  — ST/T-wave Change (Ischaemia-level changes)
              Includes NST_, ISC_, ISCA, ISCI, STD_, INVT, NT_, etc.
              ~5,200 records (~24%)

    CD    — Conduction Disturbance
              Includes LBBB, RBBB, ILBBB, IRBBB, AVB1/2/3, WPW, etc.
              ~4,700 records (~22%)

    HYP   — Hypertrophy
              Includes LVH, RVH, LAO/LAE, RAO/RAE, SEHYP, etc.
              ~2,600 records (~12%) — least frequent class

LABEL ENCODING:
  Multi-label binary vector per record: [NORM, MI, STTC, CD, HYP]
  Each element is 0 or 1. A record can have multiple 1s (multi-hot encoding).
  Confidence threshold: >= 100% (both cardiologists fully agreed).

DATA SPLIT (pre-stratified by PTB-XL authors using strat_fold):
  Training:   Folds 1–8  (~17,441 records)
  Validation: Fold 9     (~2,193 records)
  Test:       Fold 10    (~2,203 records)
  Patient-level split: all recordings from one patient stay in one fold
  (prevents data leakage from same-patient recordings across splits).

REPORT TEXT:
  Original cardiologist reports are in German.
  Machine-translated to English using GoogleTranslator (de → en)
  before ClinicalBERT tokenization.
  Column: report_en (translated), report (original German preserved)

CLASS IMBALANCE HANDLING:
  - Weighted Focal Loss: per-class positive weights
    [NORM: 1.45, MI: 4.69, STTC: 2.78, CD: 2.62, HYP: 10.48]
  - WeightedRandomSampler: oversample minority classes during training
  - Per-class AUROC and F1 reported (not just overall accuracy)

SECTION 5 — MODELS
================================================================================

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MODEL A: ECG-ONLY RESNET CLASSIFIER (checkpoints_ecg_only/)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Purpose: Fast, inference-time classifier for the web interface.
Input: 12-lead ECG signal (12 x 5000 tensor, normalized)
Output: 5 sigmoid probabilities [NORM, MI, STTC, CD, HYP]

Architecture (train_ecg_only.py / app.py — ECGResNet):
  Layer 1: ResidualBlock(12 → 64,  kernel=15, stride=2, dropout=0.1)
  Layer 2: ResidualBlock(64 → 128, kernel=7,  stride=2, dropout=0.1)
  Layer 3: ResidualBlock(128 → 192, kernel=5, stride=2, dropout=0.2)
  Layer 4: ResidualBlock(256 → 256, kernel=3, stride=2, dropout=0.2)
  Global Average Pooling → Flatten → (256,)
  Classifier Head: Linear(256→128) → BN → ReLU → Dropout(0.3) → Linear(128→5)
  Activation: Sigmoid (multi-label independent per class)

Each ResidualBlock contains:
  Conv1d → BatchNorm1d → ReLU → Dropout → Conv1d → BatchNorm1d
  Skip connection with 1x1 Conv if dimensions change.

Loss Function:
  Binary Cross-Entropy with Logits (BCEWithLogitsLoss) + per-class pos_weight

Optimizer: AdamW (lr=1e-3, weight_decay=1e-4)
Scheduler: CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts (T_0=10, T_mult=2)
Epochs: up to 50 with early stopping (patience=10)
Batch Size: 32

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MODEL B: MULTI-MODAL FUSION CLASSIFIER (checkpoints/)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Purpose: Full research model — fuses ECG signal + demographics + text.
Input: 3 modalities simultaneously
Output: 5 sigmoid probabilities

Architecture (train.py — MultiModalFusionModel):

  Encoder 1 — ECGEncoder (1D CNN, 5 blocks):
    Block 1: Conv1d(12→32,  k=15) → BN → ReLU → MaxPool1d(2) → Dropout(0.1)
    Block 2: Conv1d(32→64,  k=11) → BN → ReLU → MaxPool1d(2) → Dropout(0.1)
    Block 3: Conv1d(64→128, k=7)  → BN → ReLU → MaxPool1d(2) → Dropout(0.2)
    Block 4: Conv1d(128→192, k=5) → BN → ReLU → MaxPool1d(2) → Dropout(0.2)
    Block 5: Conv1d(192→256, k=3) → BN → ReLU → AdaptiveAvgPool1d(1)
    Output: (B, 256)

  Encoder 2 — DemographicEncoder (MLP):
    Input: 6 features [age, sex, height, weight, height_missing, weight_missing]
    Linear(6→32) → ReLU → Dropout(0.3) → Linear(32→64) → ReLU → Dropout(0.3)
    Output: (B, 64)

  Encoder 3 — TextProjector (ClinicalBERT projection):
    Input: 768-dim ClinicalBERT [CLS] embedding (pre-cached)
    Linear(768→128) → LayerNorm(128) → ReLU → Dropout(0.3)
    Output: (B, 128)

  Fusion:
    Concatenate: [256 + 64 + 128] = (B, 448)
    Linear(448→256) → BN → ReLU → Dropout(0.3)
    → Linear(256→128) → ReLU → Dropout(0.3)
    → Linear(128→5)   [logits, sigmoid applied after]

Loss Function: Focal Loss (gamma=2.0) with per-class alpha weights
Sampler: WeightedRandomSampler (weight = max pos_weight of active classes)

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
MODEL C: ECG REPORT GENERATOR — BioBART (checkpoints_report_gen/)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Purpose: Natural language ECG report generation.
Base Model: GanjinZero/biobart-base (BioBART — biomedical BART variant)
Tokenizer: AutoTokenizer from same checkpoint

TIER 2 — Template Engine (report_templates.py):
  Pure Python deterministic system. No language model.
  Takes CNN sigmoid probabilities + thresholds.
  Fills pre-written clinical sentence slots per class.
  Severity tiers: "high" (prob >= threshold + 0.15) or "medium" (prob >= threshold)
  Output: Structured paragraph. Zero hallucination risk.

TIER 3 — BioBART Smoother:
  Input: Tier 2 template text (NOT the raw signal)
  Task: Constrained paraphrase — rephrase for natural flow, no new findings added
  Generation: beam search (num_beams=4), max_length=128, no_repeat_ngram_size=3
  Post-processing: truncation artifact repair (_fix_smoothed_output),
                   text cleanup (_clean_text)
  Output: Clinically equivalent but more naturally phrased report

LEGACY — BioBART Free Generation:
  Input: Raw ECG signal → ECGBackbone → projected to BioBART encoder space
  Projection: Linear(256→768) → LayerNorm(768) → GELU
  Generation: beam search (num_beams=4), max_length=64
  Purpose: Research/ablation comparison ONLY. Not used for clinical decisions.
           Known to produce fluent but sometimes hallucinated findings.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
XAI MODELS (Explainability)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Grad-CAM (GradCAM1D):
  Applied to: last ResidualBlock of ECGResNet
  Computes: gradient of target class logit w.r.t. final conv feature maps
  Output: 1D heatmap overlaid on ECG waveform (red = high importance regions)

Integrated Gradients (compute_lead_saliency):
  Steps: 30 interpolation steps from zero baseline to input signal
  Output: Per-lead importance score (%) summing to 100%
  Shows: Which of the 12 leads was most informative for the prediction

SECTION 6 — TECHNOLOGIES
================================================================================

PROGRAMMING LANGUAGE:
  Python 3.10+

DEEP LEARNING:
  PyTorch 2.x
    - torch.nn (Conv1d, Linear, BatchNorm1d, LayerNorm, GELU, etc.)
    - torch.optim (AdamW)
    - torch.optim.lr_scheduler (CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts)
    - torch.utils.data (Dataset, DataLoader, WeightedRandomSampler)
  Transformers (Hugging Face)
    - BartForConditionalGeneration (BioBART base)
    - AutoTokenizer
    - ClinicalBERT (emilyalsentzer/Bio_ClinicalBERT) — used for text embeddings

NLP / TEXT:
  ClinicalBERT: Pre-trained on MIMIC-III clinical notes. Used to encode
                the English-translated ECG report into a 768-dim [CLS] vector.
  BioBART: Biomedical variant of BART. Used for report generation and smoothing.
  deep-translator: GoogleTranslator for German → English report translation.

SIGNAL PROCESSING:
  wfdb: WFDB Python library for reading .dat/.hea WFDB-format ECG files
  scipy.signal.resample: resampling uploaded signals to 5000 samples if needed
  NumPy: signal normalization, array operations

DATA SCIENCE / ML:
  pandas: CSV loading and data manipulation
  scikit-learn:
    - roc_auc_score (AUROC per class and macro-averaged)
    - f1_score (per class and macro)
    - precision_recall_curve (for optimal threshold finding)

VISUALIZATION:
  matplotlib: 12-lead ECG plot with Grad-CAM heatmap overlay (imshow + plot)
  Base64 encoding: ECG images returned as base64 strings in JSON API response

WEB BACKEND:
  Flask: Python web framework serving the API and HTML templates
  Routes:
    GET  /            → Main web interface
    POST /demo        → Random test patient analysis
    GET  /patients/<class> → List test patients by class
    POST /analyze/<id>     → Analyze specific ECG by ID
    POST /predict          → Upload and analyze custom .dat/.hea file

WEB FRONTEND:
  HTML5 + Vanilla CSS + JavaScript
  No external JS framework (vanilla fetch API for AJAX calls)
  Features: real-time probability bars, ECG image display, report tab switching,
            patient browser with class filter, file upload panel

METRICS / EVALUATION:
  AUROC (Area Under ROC Curve) — per class and macro average
  F1 Score — per class and macro average
  ROUGE-L — for report generation quality vs cardiologist reference
  Hamming Loss — fraction of individual label errors
  Exact Match Accuracy — all 5 labels correct simultaneously
  Hallucination Analysis — term-level comparison vs ground truth

TRAINING INFRASTRUCTURE:
  Device: CPU (torch.device("cpu")) — GPU-optional
  Checkpoint format: .pt files (PyTorch state dict + optimizer + scheduler state)
  Resume support: automatic checkpoint loading from last_checkpoint.pt
  Early stopping: patience=10 epochs on validation macro AUROC

SECTION 7 — START TO END: FULL PIPELINE WALKTHROUGH
================================================================================

PHASE 1 — DATA PREPARATION (prepare_data.py)
  1. Load ptbxl_database.csv (21,837 rows)
  2. Load scp_statements.csv (SCP code → superclass mapping)
  3. Parse scp_codes column (string dict → Python dict per row)
  4. Apply confidence threshold (>= 100%) to filter to clean labels
  5. Map surviving codes to 5 superclasses
  6. Apply NORM correction (drop NORM if pathological class also present)
  7. Exclude records with no surviving superclass label
  8. Translate German reports to English (GoogleTranslator)
  9. Assign train/val/test using strat_fold (1-8 / 9 / 10)
  10. Cache normalized 500Hz signals as .npy files (signals_cache/)
  11. Cache ClinicalBERT [CLS] embeddings as .npy files (text_cache/)
  12. Compute and save normalization stats (norm_stats.json)
  13. Save train.csv, val.csv, test.csv

PHASE 2 — MODEL TRAINING

  A. ECG-Only ResNet Classifier (train_ecg_only.py)
     - Trains 1D ResNet on ECG signals only
     - Focal Loss + WeightedRandomSampler
     - CosineAnnealingWarmRestarts scheduler
     - Saves best_model.pt to checkpoints_ecg_only/

  B. Multi-Modal Fusion Model (train.py)
     - Trains CNN + MLP + TextProjector + Fusion
     - Same loss and scheduler strategy
     - Saves best_model.pt to checkpoints/

  C. BioBART Report Generator (train_report_gen.py)
     - Trains ECGBackbone + Linear projection → BioBART decoder
     - Task: generate English ECG report from signal features
     - Loss: cross-entropy on token sequences (teacher forcing)
     - Saves best_model.pt to checkpoints_report_gen/

PHASE 3 — EVALUATION
  - evaluate.py / evaluate_hybrid.py: test set AUROC, F1, ROUGE-L
  - deep_audit_export.py: generate full audit comparison file (1711 records)
  - analyze_audit.py: parse audit file → statistical summary

PHASE 4 — INFERENCE (app.py / predict.py)
  - Load ECGResNet + ECGReportModel at startup
  - For each request:
    1. Load or receive ECG signal
    2. Normalize signal
    3. Run ResNet → 5 probabilities + thresholds → detected classes
    4. Run Grad-CAM → temporal heatmap
    5. Run Integrated Gradients → lead importance
    6. Build Tier 2 template report (report_templates.py)
    7. Run BioBART smoother on template text (Tier 3)
    8. Run BioBART free generation (Legacy, optional)
    9. Build ECG plot with Grad-CAM overlay (matplotlib → base64)
    10. Return JSON with all outputs to frontend

PHASE 5 — WEB INTERFACE DISPLAY
  - 12-lead ECG image with Grad-CAM heatmap
  - Probability bar chart per class
  - Detected/not-detected status per class
  - Lead importance ranked list
  - Report tabs: Template | Smoothed | Legacy
  - Ground-truth comparison (test set browser mode)
  - Side-by-side model report vs cardiologist reference

SECTION 9 — KEY RESEARCH DECISIONS AND JUSTIFICATIONS
================================================================================

1. WHY 5 SUPERCLASSES?
   PTB-XL has 71 SCP codes. Training a 71-class model is impractical with
   available compute and data. The 5 superclasses (NORM, MI, STTC, CD, HYP)
   are the standard groupings defined by the PTB-XL authors and used by all
   major benchmarks on this dataset.

2. WHY CONFIDENCE >= 100% FOR LABELS?
   Lower thresholds include uncertain diagnoses in training labels, adding noise.
   At 100%, both annotating cardiologists fully agreed — highest label quality.

3. WHY SIGMOID, NOT SOFTMAX?
   Multi-label classification: a patient can have MI AND HYP simultaneously.
   Softmax forces mutual exclusivity (probabilities sum to 1), which is wrong.
   Sigmoid treats each class independently (correct for multi-label).

4. WHY FOCAL LOSS?
   Standard BCE loss is dominated by the majority class (NORM).
   Focal Loss down-weights easy examples and focuses training on hard/rare cases,
   particularly improving HYP detection.

5. WHY TEMPLATE ENGINE FOR CLINICAL REPORTS?
   Free-text generation (even from biomedical LLMs) hallucinate findings.
   A template that fills slots based on CNN outputs can ONLY say what the
   classifier detected — eliminating the hallucination vector entirely.

6. WHY KEEP THE LEGACY BIOBART MODEL?
   For ablation comparison in the research paper. The Legacy model shows that
   even fine-tuned biomedical NLP models hallucinate in clinical ECG tasks,
   justifying the Tier 2 classifier-first approach as the safer alternative.

7. WHY INTEGRATED GRADIENTS FOR LEAD IMPORTANCE?
   Grad-CAM is applied to the feature map (temporal). Integrated Gradients
   attributes importance to input channels (leads). Together they provide
   both WHEN and WHERE the model looked — complementary XAI perspectives.

8. WHY PRECOMPUTE CLINICALBERT EMBEDDINGS?
   ClinicalBERT forward pass is expensive per batch. Precomputing once per
   record and caching as .npy files reduces training time significantly.
