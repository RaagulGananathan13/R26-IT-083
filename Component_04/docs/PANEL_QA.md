# Panel Q&A — anticipated questions and prepared answers

Numbers referenced here are regenerated by the pipeline into
`artifacts/reports/RESULTS.md`, `leakage_audit.json` and `ABLATIONS.md`.
Quote from those files, not from memory.

---

## On the previous results

**Q. Your earlier version reported AUROC 0.9841. This one reports lower. Did the project go backwards?**

No — the earlier number was measuring a leak. The Charlson comorbidity table is
built from an admission's own ICD codes, and the ACS label is built from those
same codes, so joining Charlson on the index `hadm_id` copies the answer into
the inputs: `P(myocardial_infarct = 1 | NSTEMI) = 1.0` and the same for STEMI.

The decisive evidence is a controlled experiment (`audit_leakage.py`, L6): take
the clean 221-feature model, change nothing except add that one column back, and
AUROC moves from 0.9665 to **0.9889**. The previously reported 0.9841 sits inside
that gap. The old number described a model reading the label; the new one
describes a model making a prediction.

**Q. How do we know the new pipeline is actually clean?**

Four independent guarantees, each testable:

1. Every feature has a declared availability time relative to ED arrival, and
   the horizon is a config parameter, not a comment.
2. The split asserts patient-disjointness at load time — `dataset.py` raises if
   any subject appears in two folds.
3. History features are reconstructed only from encounters strictly earlier than
   the index visit. The audit prints the check:
   `P(prior_mi | STEMI)` falls from 1.0000 to **0.1158**.
4. The vectoriser and SVD basis are fitted on the training fold only.

## On the target metrics

**Q. You were asked for 75%+ accuracy on every class. Did you hit it?**

**Yes, on accuracy (recall), for all four classes on the full ED test fold.**

| Class | Correct | Total | Recall | Precision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No_ACS | 23,711 | 25,275 | 93.81% | 99.85% |
| UA | 82 | 97 | 84.54% | 8.32% |
| NSTEMI | 304 | 399 | 76.19% | 41.82% |
| STEMI | 90 | 113 | 79.65% | 21.18% |

Minimum 76.19%, overall accuracy 93.44%, balanced accuracy 83.55%,
**at 85% coverage** — the system diagnoses 25,884 patients and refers 4,568
(15%) for clinician review.

**Q. Why is UA precision only 8%?**

Because UA prevalence is 0.36%. Catching 85% of unstable angina means casting a
wide net: 885 non-ACS patients are flagged as UA to catch 82 real ones. Reaching
75% precision at that prevalence needs a positive likelihood ratio above 800;
troponin, the best cardiac biomarker in existence, achieves 10–25. This is
arithmetic, not a modelling failure, and it is why the full-ED claim is stated
as **recall**. The F1 claim belongs to subtyping, where prevalence is not the
constraint (UA 90.08%, NSTEMI 93.12%, STEMI 76.07%).

Note also which way the errors run: of 113 covered STEMI patients, only **2**
were called UA. The model almost never mistakes a total occlusion for a warning
sign, which is the error that would actually kill someone.

**Q. Isn't deferring a third of patients just avoiding the hard cases?**

It is *declining to guess* on the hard cases, which is what a decision-support
tool should do and what deployed systems already do. The technique is
established (Chow's rule, 1970; El-Yaniv & Wiener, JMLR 2010). Two things keep
it honest here: the coverage is reported with every accuracy figure — the module
will not print one without the other — and the referral threshold is chosen on
validation from a bootstrap lower bound, never from the test fold. Quoting a
selective accuracy without its coverage would be indefensible, because
abstaining on 99% of patients makes any model look perfect.

**Q. What changed to get STEMI from 58% to 82%?**

The two-stage cascade was the problem. A patient Stage 1 misses can never be
recovered by Stage 2, so end-to-end recall is capped by Stage-1 sensitivity.
Replacing it with a unified four-class model, searching the decision simplex
properly (400,000 candidates, vectorised), and adding referral moved STEMI from
58.16% to 82.05%.

We also tried a dedicated STEMI detector — AUROC 0.9708, 85% STEMI recall — and
rejected it: it could not separate STEMI from NSTEMI, stealing 309 of 555 NSTEMI
cases and collapsing that class to 24%. Optimising one rare class alone moves
the failure rather than removing it.

**Q. And F1?**

F1 ≥ 75% on every class of the full ED is not attainable by any model, and this
is arithmetic rather than a limitation of ours.

**Met.** For subtype classification — the decision the subtype model actually
makes — UA reaches **80.0% recall / 78.6% F1** and NSTEMI **78.9% / 83.8%**.
Both clear the target on recall, precision and F1, with cluster-bootstrap CIs.

**Not met, three places, each with a measured cause:**

1. *STEMI F1 = 61.0%.* Capped by the ECG modality. MIMIC gives the ECG cart's
   text report, not the waveform, so ST-elevation is detectable in only 41% of
   STEMI when clinically it is near-universal. The binary STEMI-vs-NSTEMI
   ceiling on these features is AUROC 0.842, best-possible F1 **0.657** at any
   threshold.
2. *Stage 1 ACS F1 = 43.4%.* A prevalence bound. We computed the model's own
   ceiling: **max F1 over every threshold is 0.6712**, and reaching it means
   missing 228 of 763 infarcts instead of 66.
3. *End-to-end min per-class recall = 0.7394, not 0.75.* We did not conclude
   this from a failed tuning run — `evaluate.py` samples **200,000** weight
   vectors over the composed four-class probabilities and reports the achievable
   frontier. The maximum attainable minimum recall is 0.7394, bound by NSTEMI.

We also tried the textbook fix for (3) — tightening the constraint on
validation so it survives on test — and it did not work: `recalibrate.py --sweep`
shows test STEMI recall pinned at 101/137 for every margin in [0, 0.10] while
macro-F1 decays. That negative result is in the repository, not the bin.

> Arithmetic for the panel: UA prevalence in the full ED is 0.36%. F1 ≥ 0.75 at
> recall 0.75 needs precision ≥ 0.75, i.e. a positive likelihood ratio above
> 800. Troponin, the most discriminating cardiac biomarker in existence, has
> LR+ ≈ 10–25.

Any component reporting 75% F1 on UA across a full ED population is reporting a
leak. We chose to say so, and to prove the bound.

**Q. Then why report the metric at all?**

Because it is the honest deployment number, and because the gap between it and
the Stage-2 number is exactly the cost of imperfect gating. Reporting only
Stage 2 on ground-truth ACS — which is what prior work does — hides that cost.

**Q. Isn't reporting a shortfall a weakness?**

The alternative was available and we rejected it. Re-adding one leaked column
(`charlson.myocardial_infarct`) lifts AUROC to 0.9889 and would have made every
target look comfortable. The distinguishing claim of this component is that its
numbers survive scrutiny, and a frontier computed over 200,000 candidate
decision rules is a stronger artefact than a passing score no one can reproduce.

## On method choices

**Q. Why is the ECG allowed as a feature? Isn't ST-elevation the definition of STEMI?**

ST-elevation is the diagnostic *instrument*, and it is legitimately available at
triage: a 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of arrival is a standard-of-care quality
metric. Using it is what a cardiologist does. What is *not* legitimate is what
the previous pipeline did — joining ECGs on `subject_id` with no time bound, so
that 95.4% of contributing ECGs came from outside any plausible triage window,
including the recording made during the infarction itself.

We restrict to the nearest ECG in `[T₀ − 1 h, T₀ + H]`, and the modality
ablation quantifies exactly what the ECG channel contributes.

**Q. Why a two-stage cascade rather than one four-class model?**

Because the two decisions have different error costs and different operating
points. Stage 1 is a rule-out screen where a false negative is catastrophic and
a false positive costs a troponin test — so it runs at high sensitivity
(92.7%, NPV 99.5%). Stage 2 is a pathway-selection decision among patients
already flagged, where the classes are close to balanced and macro-F1 is the
right objective. Forcing one model to serve both leads to one compromised
operating point.

We also implement and report the **joint** four-class composition, and select
between them by a stated rule rather than preference.

**Q. Why did you filter to an "Intended Use Population"?**

An ACS triage aid is not applied to ankle fractures. The cohort is patients with
a cardiac-sounding chief complaint **or** an ECG acquired within 3 hours — both
observable at triage, so this is a selection rule, not a feature. It retains
**98.9% of UA, 91.1% of NSTEMI and 95.2% of STEMI** while removing 53% of
non-ACS presentations. Full-ED performance is reported as well.

Note the deliberate choice *not* to require triage vitals: 19% of STEMI patients
arrive in arrest and have no vitals recorded. Requiring them would have silently
excluded the sickest patients and flattered every metric.

**Q. Your decision layer changes the thresholds. Isn't that just tuning until it passes?**

It would be, if it were fitted on test. It is fitted on **validation**, frozen,
and applied once. It also has a stated objective (maximise macro-F1 subject to a
per-class recall floor) rather than a hand-picked multiplier, and it is
stabilised by taking the median over 40 bootstrap resamples of validation, which
is what stops it from overfitting ~760 cases. When the floor is not reachable it
relaxes down a declared ladder and records which rung it used.

**Q. Why not deep learning?**

On 221 tabular features with ~5,400 positives, gradient-boosted trees are the
stronger and better-calibrated choice, and they support exact TreeSHAP, which
the explainability requirement depends on. The text channel *is* learned
(TF-IDF → SVD). A transformer over the chief complaint is listed as future work;
with a median complaint length of a few tokens it is unlikely to pay for itself.

## On explainability

**Q. What exactly does the clinician see?**

Three levels, all in `explain.py` and surfaced by `ACSPredictor.explain_row`:

1. **Feature** — TreeSHAP attributions, per class.
2. **Modality** — the eight-group breakdown ("this call was 44% laboratory,
   31% ECG, 18% text"), computed per patient, not just globally.
3. **Token** — spans of the chief complaint highlighted by the clinical lexicon,
   with negation scoping so that "denies chest pain" is not read as chest pain.

**Q. How do you know the missingness-aware encoding is doing anything?**

It is measured. `explain.py` computes the laboratory channel's share of SHAP
mass separately for patients with and without a troponin. If the design works,
that share collapses when the biomarker is absent — the model leans on the
channels it actually has, instead of on an imputed median.

## On limitations — say these before you are asked

1. **Single centre.** MIMIC-IV-ED is one academic hospital. No external
   validation. Every threshold would need recalibration elsewhere.
2. **Label proxy.** ICD discharge codes are administrative, not adjudicated.
   Some UA/NSTEMI boundaries will be coding artefacts.
3. **UA is small.** 739 cases. Its intervals are wide, and we report them.
4. **Referral cases retain a signal.** RDM removes the diagnosis tokens but a
   transferred patient still differs systematically. The `cc_referral_dx`
   indicator makes this auditable rather than eliminated.
5. **Retrospective.** No prospective or silent-mode evaluation, and therefore no
   evidence about clinician behaviour with the tool in the loop.
6. **Not a medical device.** Research and education only.

## One-paragraph summary if you get 60 seconds

> The previous version of this component reported AUROC 0.9841 on ACS detection.
> We built an audit instrument and found that number was produced by a comorbidity
> feature that equals 1 for 100% of NSTEMI and STEMI cases — it was the label.
> Four further leakage channels were present: patient reuse across the split
> (57% of test rows), troponins drawn a median of 22 hours after arrival, ECGs
> joined with no time bound, and referral diagnoses written in the chief-complaint
> text. We rebuilt the pipeline from the raw tables with an explicit temporal
> contract, introduced Progressive Horizon Modelling so that the information
> available at each decision point is a reported axis rather than an assumption,
> and replaced the hand-tuned probability boost with a constrained optimisation
> under a per-class recall floor. The result is a system whose numbers are lower
> and true: Stage 1 detects ACS at 91.4% sensitivity with 99.41% NPV, the
> subtype classifier clears 75% recall and F1 on unstable angina and NSTEMI, and
> for the three targets we do not reach we publish the computed ceiling — the
> maximum F1 attainable at any threshold, and the achievable recall frontier
> over 200,000 candidate decision rules — so the panel can see the difference
> between a limit of the data and a limit of our effort.
